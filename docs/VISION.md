# Vision

## One-sentence pitch

Ghostrank is a hardcore hacker-life roguelite where the player survives a dangerous anonymous underground by understanding systems, building or buying tools, choosing targets, managing exposure, and knowing when not to act.

## Player fantasy

The player is not a superhero pressing a Hack button. They are an unknown handle in a hostile ecosystem.

At the beginning of a run they may be ranked around one hundred thousandth, using modest hardware in a cramped room. A highly skilled player can nevertheless attempt a top-ten target on day one. Success is possible because rank represents reputation and known history, not numerical combat power.

The fantasy is becoming dangerous because the player understands the world.

## Core emotions

- curiosity while investigating a target,
- competence when multiple clues form a usable hypothesis,
- greed when a risky opportunity appears,
- paranoia when signals suggest someone may be watching,
- relief after safely disengaging,
- dread after realizing a small earlier mistake created a causal chain,
- pride when a self-built workflow replaces an expensive tool,
- restraint when the player chooses not to take an attractive target,
- earned peace when a rare run ends in a successful disappearance.

## Audience

Primary audience:
- players who enjoy systems-heavy roguelikes,
- technical fantasy without requiring professional technical knowledge,
- immersive desktop/OS games,
- emergent stories,
- difficult games where learning transfers between runs.

Technical familiarity should provide flavor and intuition but must not be a prerequisite. The game teaches its own fictional systems.

## What Ghostrank is

- single-player,
- simulation-driven,
- run-based,
- difficult,
- primarily computer-interface gameplay inside an inhabited, customizable fictional OS,
- visually legible systems that favor recognition before dense reading,
- supported by a small physical-world slice,
- modern in its treatment of AI, hardware, crypto-like economies, and software ecosystems,
- designed for emergent stories instead of one fixed campaign.

## What Ghostrank is not

- a real hacking tutorial,
- a terminal-only game,
- a wall of terminal-styled menus, buttons, and text,
- a clicker with percentage-based hacking,
- a level-gated RPG,
- a morality-meter game,
- a cybersecurity job simulator,
- an open-world walking game,
- a multiplayer MMO,
- a realistic implementation of real criminal infrastructure.

## White and Black starts

A new run may begin from a White-side or Black-side starting package.

This controls initial:
- contacts,
- contracts,
- tools,
- software access,
- social trust,
- hardware/setup flavor,
- available marketplaces and information.

It does not lock actions.

A White-start player can steal. A Black-start player can hunt criminals. Repeated behavior changes how factions and individuals describe and treat the handle.

The White fantasy is a dangerous anonymous vigilante who targets predatory and criminal actors rather than a corporate pentester.

The Black fantasy is a profit-first operator who treats banks, corporations, wealthy individuals, other hackers, and criminal targets as opportunities according to risk and reward.

## Learning curve

Runs are expected to be short at first. Reaching day four on an early run can be meaningful progress. Reaching ten days should feel earned. Exceptional experienced runs may last dozens of days.

Meta-progression should primarily exist in the player's brain. Permanent unlocks, if any, must not erase the danger or become substitutes for mastery.

## Ethical tone

The game depicts a morally compromised underground without presenting every illegal outcome as equivalent.

The rare peaceful White retirement ending should feel earned by a history of targeting harmful actors and redirecting value toward victims or constructive projects. It should not be framed as a tutorial for concealing real proceeds.

Black paths may also retire, but their endings can retain tension, paranoia, unresolved enemies, or moral cost.

The game can be dark without becoming celebratory about real-world harm.
