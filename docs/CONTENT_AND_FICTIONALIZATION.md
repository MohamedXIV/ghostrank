# Content and Fictionalization

## Goal

Ghostrank should feel technically coherent without becoming a usable real-world intrusion or evasion manual.

We use recognizable concepts, then fictionalize operational detail.

## Fictionalize

Prefer fictional or abstracted:
- vulnerable services,
- exploit chains,
- malware behavior,
- command syntax,
- credential attacks,
- evasion mechanics,
- anonymization infrastructure,
- marketplaces,
- criminal organizations,
- digital currencies,
- quantum-access services,
- law-enforcement agencies where useful.

Real terms can appear for broad computing literacy, but exact harmful procedures are not required for good simulation design.

## Terminal language

The terminal may resemble familiar shells but commands should target the virtual capability API.

It must never access:
- the user's real shell,
- unrelated local files,
- stored browser credentials,
- real remote hosts,
- the real internet as an attack surface.

## Generated software

AI-generated in-game software is virtual workflow/IR.

Do not:
- eval arbitrary generated JavaScript,
- invoke real compilers on untrusted in-game content,
- execute downloaded NPC packages as host binaries,
- expose unrestricted Tauri filesystem/shell/network commands to in-game tools.

## Content tone

The underground includes:
- fraud,
- extortion,
- predatory markets,
- organized crime,
- vigilantism,
- corporate targets,
- rivalry,
- betrayal,
- physical danger.

Depict consequences and trade-offs rather than turning every harmful act into a celebratory reward.

## White-side content

White-side play is not corporate compliance work.

It can involve anonymous vigilantes targeting:
- scams,
- extortion groups,
- predatory marketplaces,
- traffickers in fictionalized contexts,
- criminal financial networks,
- dangerous hackers,
- actors who violate underground survival norms.

The player can still behave badly; the simulation reacts.

## Black-side content

Black-side play is profit-first rather than automatically sadistic.

Targets may include:
- corporations,
- banks in fictional form,
- wealthy individuals,
- rival hackers,
- other criminal actors.

Physical violence is not the player's core verb, even though some organizations in the world may respond physically.

## Phase 8 authoring boundary

Phase 8 moves authored peer/target/infrastructure data into a dedicated content
roster. Content entries may carry **editorial archetype tags** (for example
broker, automation specialist, scam/extortion/predatory-market or corporate
target families in later slices), but those tags are not simulation classes.

The runtime receives only the existing mechanic-bearing actor/target data:
network, skill, defense, discretion, activity, optional assistant, public
identity, machines and fictional services. Archetype metadata must not become a
rank substitute, target-level gate, hidden bonus, or persisted player-facing
fact.

Authored content is validated for coherent ids/references and cloned before a
run can mutate it. This lets Phase 8 broaden fictionalized content without
introducing special-case runtime branches for named characters.

## G9 fictional institutions and systemic content authoring

Fictional crises may involve major organizations, oppressive institutions, corruption, leaks, victim protection, influence campaigns and even multi-state repercussions. **Scale is not a rank level gate or morality meter.** Define fictional actors/institutions, particular witnesses, agendas, resources and sourced evidence rather than importing real credentials, private persons or operational attack instructions.

A content template requires a real producer, earned versus reported source semantics, optional approaches including non-cooperation/walking away, and justified NPC knowledge. A broker contract, independent player investigation and NPC-origin incident may bind to the same simulation mechanism. Do not label dramatic prose as a systemic consequence: model a bounded world domain before claiming a fictional national institution changed.

Ink/inkjs may author fictional conversations, rumors, reports, decisions and multi-step narrative arcs, but story assets never execute host/network code, write WorldState, expose private truth or bypass typed simulation commands. Validate IDs, cross-references, projection privacy and current-run story version. See `docs/SEMANTIC_CONTENT_AUTHORING.md` and epic #135. These features are planned, not shipped.

## Research policy

When drawing inspiration from real cybersecurity:
- research concepts, incentives, historical patterns, and defensive reasoning,
- translate them into fictional systems,
- avoid copying operational exploit steps into player-facing content.

The game should reward system thinking, not real credential theft or real intrusion technique memorization.
